# P(-1) review — 2026-10-31 (2.8.0, of the 2.7.0 changes)

**Scope.** What 2.7.0 changed in `src/` (`git diff 2.6.0 2.7.0 -- src/`):
- **DSP:** the six new windows (symmetric and periodic), the window metrics,
  and the RBJ biquads (`biquad_design`, `biquad_response`, `biquad_process`).
- **Evaluator:** `mean`'s rewritten overflow path, `stddev` on a double-double
  mean, and the argument cap's PARSE / ARITY rule.

The review of 2.6.0 is [`2026-10-31-fix-audit.md`](2026-10-31-fix-audit.md).

**Method.** One reviewer, in a scratch copy of 2.7.0 (Cyrius 6.7.18), briefed
to report only what it reproduced, against references independent of the
code: Python `fractions`, 60-digit `decimal`, and numpy. It also used an
instrumented copy, with counters for out-of-bounds indexes and for expansion
lengths, and a 2.6.0 baseline. It was told what the 2.7.0 release had already
checked, so it spent its time on edges and extremes. Every finding was
reproduced again by hand before it was fixed. Each fix came with tests that
fail on 2.7.0, and the suite was run after each.

**Result.** 3 low and 6 info. All three low findings are fixed in code, and
so are two of the info items (5 and part of 7). The rest are documented.

| # | Severity | Area | Finding | Regression? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | low | eval | `stddev` read 16 bytes below its buffer on a trailing Inf / NaN | from 2.7.0 |
| 2 | low | dsp | `biquad_design` returned Ok for designs with a pole on or outside the unit circle | new in 2.7.0 |
| 3 | low | dsp | the window metrics overflowed or flushed on scaled buffers | new in 2.7.0 |
| 4 | info | dsp | `biquad_process` ends in a subnormal limit cycle, never 0 | — |
| 5 | info | dsp | biquad coefficients near fs/4 and fs/2 were up to 77,269 ulp off | new in 2.7.0 |
| 6 | info | dsp | `biquad_response`'s error at a resonance grows with Q | — |
| 7 | info | dsp, eval | documentation gaps; an unknown name past the cap reported ARITY | no |

## 1 — `stddev` read below its buffer (low)

`_eval_stddev` added every argument to the exact-sum expansion. An Inf or NaN
leaves the expansion at length −1. `_mean_rem` then called
`_xp_round(ps, -1)`, which reads `ps[-2]`, 16 bytes below the 528-byte stack
array.

The reviewer's instrumented copy counted one such read each for
`stddev(1, Inf)`, `stddev(Inf)`, `stddev(NaN)`, `stddev(1, 2, NaN)` and
`stddev(-Inf, 3, Inf)`. I reproduced it with my own counter. The read is
read-only and stays inside the mapped stack, and the result was NaN anyway.
There was no write out of bounds: `_xp_add` at length −1 writes only `ps[0]`.

**Fix.** A non-finite argument returns NaN before any summing, as `_eval_mean`
already did. It is the canonical `0x7FF8…` on every target; through 2.7.0 it
was whatever the hardware made of Inf − Inf, `0xFFF8…` on x86. The counter
now reads 0. `test_stats_robust` adds three rows, each of which gives the old
NaN bits at 2.7.0.

## 2 — `biquad_design` returned unstable sections (low)

Nothing checked the rounded coefficients, so at extreme parameters a pole
landed on or past the unit circle and the design still came back Ok:

| Design | Coefficients | Exact margin |
|---|---|---|
| low-pass, f0 = 5e-5 Hz, fs 48 kHz, q 1 | a1 = −0x1.ffffffe3e3b6ap+0, a2 = 0x1.ffffffc7c76d3p-1 | 1 + a1 + a2 = −2^-53: a real pole above 1. The correctly rounded a1 is 1 ulp away, and stable. |
| peak, 1 kHz, q 1/√2, −700 dB | a2 = −1.0 exactly | 1 + a1 + a2 = −6.8e-17 |
| peak, 1 kHz, q 1/√2, +700 dB | a2 = +1.0 | poles on the circle; the step response still rings at ±4e17 after 60,000 samples |
| low-pass, 1 kHz, q 1e100 | a2 = 1.0 | poles on the circle |

The reviewer's scans found the problem from f0/fs ≈ 6.4e-9, and from a gain
of ~449 dB (poles on the circle) or ~517 dB (outside). The shelves at large
negative gains degenerate too: at −20,000 dB, b = 0, a1 = ±2 and a2 = 1.

**Fix.** The stability triangle, |a2| < 1 and |a1| < 1 + a2 (Jury), is checked
on the stored coefficients, and a design that fails it is Err. The second
inequality is evaluated as |a1| − a2 < 1, which is exact near its edge
(Sterbenz), where 1 + a2 could round. Every Ok section now has both poles
strictly inside the circle. `test_biquad_design_errors` adds five rows, each
Ok at 2.7.0. `test_biquad_stability`'s 20,000 valid designs are all still Ok.

## 3 — the window metrics at extreme scales (low)

The doc said the definitions "apply to any buffer", but the squares and sums
did not survive scale:

| Buffer | Metric | 2.7.0 | Exact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 × 1e154 | ENBW | NaN | 1 |
| periodic Hann(64) × 1e154 | ENBW | NaN | 1.5 |
| 8 × 1e-160 | ENBW | 0.99999228 | 1 |
| 4 × 5e-324 | ENBW | NaN | 1 |
| 4 × 5e-324 | scalloping loss | 2.4988 dB | 3.6980 dB |
| [DBL_MAX, DBL_MAX] | coherent gain | +Inf | DBL_MAX |
| [DBL_MAX, DBL_MAX] | ENBW, scalloping loss | NaN | finite |

**Fix.** Each metric first runs on w itself, as before. Only when a sum comes
out non-finite, or below 2^-700 where terms may have flushed to subnormals,
does it run again on w·2^-e, with the largest |w| in [1, 2). The gain needs
the rerun only on overflow, since a sum of subnormals is exact.
- The scaling is an exact power of two, applied in at most two steps.
- ENBW and scalloping loss are ratios, so the scaling does not change them;
  the gain is (Σ/n)·2^e.
- Ordinary windows take the first path and get the same bits as before.

A first cut always scaled, behind a separate pass to find the largest |w|.
That cost `window_enbw_1024` 47%; the check on the sums costs about 2%. `test_window_metrics_scale` checks
periodic Hann(64)·2^k for k = −900 to 1000 bit for bit, plus the rows above;
7 of its 8 assertions fail at 2.7.0.

## 4 — `biquad_process`'s subnormal tail (info)

A decaying tail never reaches 0. A 1 kHz low-pass after one impulse settles in
a limit cycle with state (−1e-323, +1e-323). 8,184,369 of 8,192,000 outputs
were subnormal, which cost ×1.55 on Zen 3 and would cost more where
subnormals are microcoded. Flushing the state inside the loop would break the
promise that consecutive blocks chain bit for bit. **Documented** instead: zero
the state, or stop processing, once the input has gone silent.

## 5 — biquad coefficients near fs/4 and fs/2 (info)

w0 = 2π·f0/fs carries an error of ~ulp(w0). A value near 0 cannot absorb that,
and three of them sit near 0 at the edges of the band: cos w0 near fs/4, and
sin w0 and cos(w0/2) near fs/2. Measured per coefficient, in ulps of itself
(the 2.7.0 figure of 3.6 ulp was in ulps of max(|c|, 1), which hid this):
- the high-pass b0 at 23,999.9 Hz (fs 48 kHz) was 77,269 ulp off;
- a peak's b1 at 11,999 Hz was 9,465 ulp off;
- the band-pass b0 at 23,990 Hz was ~500 ulp off.

The comment claimed the near-fs/2 formulas did not cancel.

**Fix.** `_bq_trig` takes each angle from the nearest of 0, π/2 and π. The
offsets π/2 − w0 and π − w0 are formed from exact frequency differences,
fs/4 − f0 and fs/2 − f0 (Sterbenz), as `biquad_response` already does for π.

| Region | 2.7.0 worst | 2.8.0 worst |
|---|---:|---:|
| near DC (f0 ≤ fs/8) | 2.4 ulp | 2.4 ulp |
| around fs/4 | 9,465 ulp | 244 ulp |
| near fs/2 (f0 > 3fs/8) | 77,269 ulp | 2.4 ulp |

The 244 that remains is a peaking b2 = (1 − αA)/a0 at αA ≈ 1, where the
cookbook formula itself cancels to near 0; its absolute error is about ε.
Over the 1,135-design corpus the worst coefficient error falls from 3.6 to
2.1 ulp of max(|c|, 1). `test_biquad_coeffs` adds seven rows; 4 fail at
2.7.0.

## 6 — `biquad_response` at a resonance (info)

At f0 the denominator shrinks to ~1/Q, so the error grows with Q in every
branch, even measured at the code's own rounded angle. Over 5,740 in-band
points, with the notch zeros excluded, the worst per Q was:

| Q | 0.707 | 20 | 50 | 200 | 1,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| worst | 3.9 ulp | 41 | 206 | 689 | 3,151 (~1e-12 relative) |

2.7.0's p99 of 5.5 ulp held for its low-Q corpus. **Documented** in the
function's comment and in `docs/sources.md`.

## 7 — documentation and one error code (info)

**Fixed in code:**
- **An unknown name past the cap.** `nosuch(<33 args>)` reported ARITY; it is
  UNKNOWN_FN now, as below the cap. The behaviour was the same in 2.6.0.
  `test_arity_cap` adds a row.

**Documentation corrected:**
- **Flat-top.** Its interior goes as low as −0.0706, not only its ends; this
  matches scipy.
- **The Err threshold.** "Gains past ~12,000 dB" held for the peak only. The
  shelves overflowed from ~6,200 dB and never refused negative gains. Finding
  2's check now covers all of them.
- **Huge f0.** An f0 past ~2.86e307 is refused, because 2π·f0 overflows.
- **`Evaluator_eval_partial`.** Its over-cap open calls back off as other open
  calls do: `2+mean(<33 ones>` gives 2 / NONE, where 2.6.0 gave ARITY. Noted
  in the upgrade guide.

## Checked and sound

- **Windows at extreme sizes:** 2,912 cases against 60-digit Decimal.
  - Sizes 2–5, 2^31, 2^32+1, 2^53±1, 2^53+2, 2^62, 2^62+1 and 2^63−1, for all
    14 generators; n at the ends and middle.
  - Worst error in ulps of the peak: 0.76 for the cosine sums, 0.5 for
    Bartlett, 1.0 for Tukey, 0.37 for Gaussian. No integer overflow.
  - 616 of 616 symmetric pairs were bit-exact.
- **Tukey and Gaussian parameters:** 44,736 cases (subnormal, ±0, ±1 ± 2^-52,
  huge, ±Inf, NaN), all within 1.07 ulp of the peak and in [0, 1].
- **Index and size edges:** 630 cases with INT64_MIN and MAX give 0, or 1 for
  size 1.
- **Metric accuracy:** 53 buffers with n = 1–10,000.
  - The gain is within 0.74 ulp and the ENBW within 2 ulp.
  - The scalloping loss is within 15 ulp; near 0 dB the value is
    ill-conditioned, and its absolute error is under 2e-15 dB.
- **`biquad_design` refusals:** 44,352 malformed inputs. `out` was untouched
  on every Err, and nothing was written past 40 bytes.
- **`biquad_response`:** 9,720 points and 96 special (f, fs) pairs. They
  included the branch boundaries ±1 ulp, negative f, f past fs, f up to 1e300,
  and f and fs at ±0, ±Inf, NaN and subnormal. The phase was always in
  (−π, π] or NaN.
- **`biquad_process`:** n ≤ 0 writes nothing, a NaN state gives NaN, and
  dst == src matches separate buffers.
- **`mean`:** 140,052 cases against the exact Fraction mean, 0 wrong. They
  included 40,000 forced-overflow cases at k = 2..6, sums at 2^1017 ±
  ulps ± 2^-1074, and 32-argument cases. The expansion peaked at 32 partials
  against 66 slots.
- **`stddev`:** 30,049 finite cases, worst 1.000 ulp.
- **Argument cap:**
  - 26 expressions through eval and eval_partial follow the documented
    precedence.
  - Nesting fails at the depth limit, 256.
  - The 1,024-token limit caps a call near 510 arguments.
  - Past-cap arguments cost the same as an equivalent `+` chain.
