# P(-1) review — 2026-10-31 (2.7.0, of the 2.6.0 changes)

**Scope.** What 2.6.0 changed in `src/` (`git diff 2.5.2 2.6.0 -- src/`) and
the tests and fuzz that came with it:
- the exact-sum `mean` and the corrected two-pass `stddev`;
- the argument cap's `ABACO_ERR_ARITY`;
- the number theory: `mod_inverse`, `crt`, `jacobi`, `isqrt` / `iroot` /
  `is_perfect_power`, the multiplicative functions, `primitive_root`, and the
  eight evaluator names;
- the HTTPS deadline, `sinc_kernel`'s envelope and the absolute-zero margin.

The 2.6.0 review itself is [`2026-10-31-audit.md`](2026-10-31-audit.md).

**Method.** One reviewer in a scratch copy of 2.6.0 (Cyrius 6.7.18), briefed
to report only what it reproduced, against references independent of the
code:
- Python `Fraction` sums, correctly rounded;
- Python big integers and a Pollard-rho reference;
- brute force for primitive roots;
- an instrumented copy to measure the expansion buffers.

Every finding was reproduced again by hand before it was fixed. Each fix came
with tests that fail on 2.6.0, and the suite was run after each.

## 1 — `mean`'s overflow path (low)

When a partial sum overflowed, 2.6.0 scaled every argument by 2^-k
(k = 1..6) and rounded the mean on the scaled grid. That broke the correct
rounding 2.6.0 promised in two ways:
- **Small arguments.** An argument below 2^(k−1022) lost its low bits, or all
  of them, so a tie it should break went to even.
- **Coarse grid.** A result below 2^(k−1022) after cancellation was rounded
  on a grid 2^k times too coarse. That included normal results, not only the
  subnormal ones the comment admitted.

Examples, with M = DBL_MAX:
- `mean(M, M, -M, -M, 1.7800590868057615e-307)` was 27 ulp off. The same
  arguments ordered `M, -M, M, -M, x` never overflow, and were right.
- `mean(M, M, -M, -M, 2^-1070)` was 0, not 1.5e-323.
- `mean(M, M, 2^971, -5e-324)` went to even; the −5e-324 should have taken it
  down.

The reviewer's corpus found 619 of 10,000 overflowing means wrong, up to
31 ulp on normal results.

**Fix.** Each argument is split exactly, as x = 2^k·a + b:
- a = x·2^-k, rounded;
- b = x − 2^k·a, which is nonzero only for |x| < 2^(k−1022). It lies on the
  2^-1074 grid with |b| ≤ 2^(k−1075), so B = Σb is one exact double.

The sum is S = 2^k·A + B exactly, and the mean finishes in one of two ways:
- **A below 2^(1017−k).** A unscales exactly, B joins it, and the mean
  finishes on the unscaled grid, as for any other input.
- **Otherwise.** |mean| ≥ 2^1011, so the scaled grid is the true one. Each
  sign the rounding needs is the sign of 2^k·X + y, taken exactly: X decides
  when its top partial is 2^-1000 or more, since it then outweighs y.
  Otherwise the partials scale up by 2^k and merge with y.

The reviewer suggested summing the arguments below 2^(k−1022) unscaled and
letting them only break ties. That is not enough. An argument just above the
bound can leave the scaled remainder a few ulps of 2^-1074 from a tie, and
arguments below the bound can outweigh it. `test_stats_robust` pins one such
midpoint.

**After:**
- the reviewer's 10,000 overflowing means: 0 wrong (619 at 2.6.0);
- 80,000 adversarial overflowing means, ties at the top decided by split
  arguments and cancellation beside them: 0 wrong (14,388 at 2.6.0);
- 420,000 means in seven sets over three seeds: random bits, mixed magnitudes,
  cancellation, ties, subnormal-heavy, 32 arguments, ±DBL_MAX mixtures. All
  exact.

`test_stats_robust` adds seven rows, five of them wrong at 2.6.0. The
reordered row was always right and pins order independence. The last row
pins the midpoint above.

## 2 — `stddev` below an ulp of spread (info)

2.6.0's corrected two-pass form, Σd² − (Σd)²/n, takes out the mean's rounding
error δ only while δ is small next to the spread. When the data spread less
than an ulp of the mean, both terms are about n·δ² and the subtraction
cancels. 12 copies of 3835040064.878044 and 19 of the next double up gave a
stddev 57 ulp off; six values near 9.1e13 were 89 ulp off. The reviewer
measured 358 of 13,085 such cases more than 9 ulp off.

**Fix.**
- **The mean as a double-double.** mh comes from the exact sum, and
  ml = round(R) / n from its exact remainder (the same `_mean_rem` as
  `mean`).
- **Exact deviations.** x − mh is formed exactly as a two-sum (Knuth), less
  ml. It is renormalised as e + f with |f| ≤ ulp(e)/2, and squared as
  e² + 2ef, with e² exact as a two-product.
- **Compensated Σd²** (Neumaier). The variance is then right to a few units of
  2^-104, and only v / n and the square root round.

Two earlier cuts fell short:
- **The first** took the mean as a double-double and compensated Σd², but
  rounded each deviation twice. Its worst case was 2 ulp, and 21 records of
  the differential moved 1 ulp further from the exact value than in 2.6.0.
- **The second** squared the unnormalised dh + dl as dh² + 2·dh·dl, losing
  dl², which is comparable to dh² once ml is taken off. The clustered corpus
  caught it, with results of 0.

| | 2.6.0 | 2.7.0 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2–3 adjacent doubles, 39,037 cases (two seeds): over 9 ulp | 370 (one seed) | 0 |
| the same: worst | 62 ulp | 1 ulp |
| random, clustered, constant and wide (40,000): worst | 10 ulp | 1 ulp |
| the 31-value case above | 57 ulp | exact |
| the differential's 3,188 scorable `stddev` records: exact / 1 ulp | 2,906 / 282 | 2,981 / 207 |

`stddev` is not correctly rounded, but it is within 1 ulp on every corpus.
`test_stats_robust` pins the 31-value case and 2.6.0's clustered case, both
exact.

## 3 — malformed calls past the argument cap (info)

2.6.0 reported `ABACO_ERR_ARITY` at the 33rd comma, before reading anything
after it. So the syntax error `mean(1, …, 1,)`, with 32 ones, was ARITY,
while `mean(1,)` was PARSE. **Fix.** Past the cap the remaining arguments are
still parsed, but not stored. A call that does not parse is PARSE: a trailing
comma, a malformed 34th argument, a missing `)`. Only a call that parses is
ARITY. An argument past the cap that raises its own error, such as an unknown
variable, now reports that error, left to right, as arguments before the cap
always did. `test_arity_cap` adds five rows; three of them were ARITY at
2.6.0.
`fuzz_eval` (50,000 iterations) still checks that any call past the cap that
parses gets exactly ARITY.

## Checked and sound

- **`mean` on the normal path:** 140,000 cases in the reviewer's seven sets,
  all exact. The one miss was finding 1. Signed zeros were right:
  `(-0, -0)` gives −0, `(0, -0)` +0, `(-5e-324, -0)` −0 and
  `(5e-324, -5e-324)` +0.
- **Expansion buffers** (instrumented, 120,000 cases, 18,080 through the
  overflow path):
  - the rounding steps at most once, so the 3-step bound is never reached;
  - there are at most 32 partials in `ps` and 33 in `es`, against room for
    66; the theoretical maximum is n + 5 = 37.
- **The masked q·n split:** qh·n < 2^1024, so it never overflows.
- **Number theory:**
  - μ, τ, σ and λ: 23,000 n against a Pollard-rho reference, with 0
    mismatches. The n included primorials, prime powers up to p^62, p² just
    above 8191, squares of primes near 3.04e9, values near 2^63, and σ's
    overflow boundaries.
  - `_factor_pe`: 20,008 n, exact, with at most 15 distinct primes in the
    16-slot buffers.
  - `primitive_root`: ~9,000 n against brute force, 0 mismatches. On the 30
    largest primes below 2^63, and on 2p for each, the largest root was 21.
  - `mod_inverse`, `jacobi`: 30,000 pairs each against Python, including
    i64 min and max.
  - `crt`: 30,000 systems of up to 32 moduli, including non-coprime moduli
    and lcm overflow.
  - `isqrt`, `iroot`, `is_perfect_power`: 20,000 each, plus 621 extreme
    `iroot` calls.
- **Evaluator names:** 160,000 random calls matched Python, including
  negative `modpow` exponents. Fractions, ±Inf and m ≤ 0 give
  `ABACO_ERR_MATH`.
- **The rest of 2.6.0:**
  - `sinc_kernel`: 30,000 pairs; k(x) = k(−x) bit for bit, and the edges
    are exactly 0.
  - The absolute-zero margin: 13 boundary probes.
  - The HTTPS deadline starts before the lookup, and no socket exists when
    the spent-deadline exit returns.
  - The 2.6.0 code keeps no per-call state in globals.
