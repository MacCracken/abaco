# P(-1) review — 2026-10-31 (2.6.0)

**Scope.** Everything in `src/` that changed between the 2.4.9 audit
([`2026-09-30-audit.md`](2026-09-30-audit.md)) and 2.5.2, about 1,950 changed
lines:
- the evaluator (2.4.10–2.5.2): the correctly rounded literal parser and its
  big-integer midpoint comparison, exact `n!`, the compensated `mean` and
  overflow-safe `stddev`, id dispatch and `ABACO_ERR_ARITY`, the stdlib `tan`,
  and the uncapped `totient`;
- the HTTPS currency fetch (2.4.12, reworked in 2.5.0): URL split, request
  builder, response framing, CA-file handling, the `tls_set_deadline` step
  and fetch deadlines, and the error mapping;
- number theory, DSP, units and core: Pollard–Brent rho (2.5.2),
  `abaco_binomial`, `_isqrt`, the symmetric and periodic windows, Kaiser, and
  the unit changes.

**Method.** Three reviewers, one per area, each in its own scratch copy of
2.5.2 (Cyrius 6.7.18). Each was briefed to report only what it reproduced,
with the input, the expected and actual result, and a reference independent
of the code:
- Python `fractions` / `decimal` at 200–2,000 digits, and `float(str)`;
- Python big integers, and a C replica of rho;
- 60-digit `decimal` window formulas;
- loopback TLS servers, and a fake resolver in a network namespace.

Every finding was then reproduced again by hand before it was fixed. The
fixes were made one at a time, each with a test that fails on 2.5.2, and the
suite was run after each.

**Result.** 8 findings, none critical or high: 1 medium, 4 low, 3 info. All 8
are fixed in 2.6.0. Two reviewers' cross-checks also confirmed the large
parts that changed since 2.4.9 (below).

## A — the HTTPS deadline started after DNS (low)

`_ccy_https_get` computed its 30 s deadline after `net_resolve_ipv4`. Yet
`src/ai.cyr`, the README and the 2.5.0 upgrade note promised that the whole
fetch finishes within 30 s. lib/net.cyr's resolver takes no deadline: 2 tries
of up to 8 replies, 2 s per read, about 32 s at worst. A reviewer ran a fake
resolver in a network namespace, sending a wrong-txid reply every 1.9 s and
answering on the second query, with a silent peer behind it. The fetch ended
after **38,614 ms**.

**Fix.** The deadline starts before the lookup, and a lookup that uses it up
is `AI_ERR_HTTP` once it returns. The same probe now ends at **30,003 ms**.
The lookup itself still cannot be interrupted: a silent resolver under a
300 ms deadline takes ~4 s to give up, and only then is the fetch refused. The
comments, `SECURITY.md` and the README now state that bound. A resolver that
takes a deadline is an upstream ask (`docs/development/roadmap.md`). No CI test
covers the probe, because it needs a network namespace; it was run by hand
before and after the fix.

## B, C, F — `mean` (medium, low, info)

2.4.10 summed the arguments with Neumaier's compensation, after scaling the
largest into [1, 2). Three things broke:
- **The scaling flushed small arguments.** `mean(1e308, -1e308, 1e-300)` was 0.
- **The compensation lost what large cancelling terms left.** Beside
  ±1.8e308, the compensation term grows past 1e290 and drops a 1e122
  contribution, so a mean of 7.6e120 came back 0. This was also wrong in 2.4.9
  and 2.5.2.
- **Subnormal means were rounded twice**, and a tie that only a tiny argument
  could break went to even (F).

The reviewer's corpora put 292 of 20,000 random means and 358 of 5,000
subnormal-heavy means off the exact rational mean.

**Fix: `mean` is correctly rounded in every case.**
- **Exact sum.** The sum is kept exactly as a Shewchuk expansion of
  non-overlapping partials, the algorithm behind Python's `math.fsum`
  (Shewchuk 1997).
- **Correctly rounded division.** q = round(S) / n to within about an ulp. The
  remainder R = S − q·n is formed exactly: q's top 27 significant bits times n,
  plus the rest of q times n, both exact for n ≤ 32. q then steps to its
  neighbour while 2|R| exceeds n·|q′ − q|, decided exactly, with ties to even.
  Subnormal results land on their grid in one rounding.
- **Scaling only on overflow.** The arguments are scaled down only when a
  partial sum overflows.
- **A bug caught along the way.** A first cut formed q·n with Dekker's
  `_two_product`, whose splitting multiply overflows for |q| above ~2^996. The
  corpus caught it (451 misses), and the masked split replaced it.

**After: 0 of 20,000 and 0 of 5,000.** `test_stats_robust` pins six cases, each
wrong at 2.5.2.

## D — `stddev` on clustered data (low)

The two-pass formula Σ(x − x̄)² / n feeds the mean's own rounding error δ back
as n·δ². `stddev(-837649.1643549813, -837649.1643459676)` was 443,769 ulp off.

**Fix.** The corrected two-pass form, Σd² − (Σd)²/n (Chan, Golub & LeVeque
1983). On the reviewer's 10,000 clustered cases:

| | 2.4.9 | 2.5.2 | 2.6.0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| exact | 1,056 | 4,927 | 6,135 |
| within 2 ulp | 1,546 | 8,190 | 9,458 |
| 4 ulp or more | 8,379 | 1,458 | 260 |
| worst | ~2.3e18 ulp | 443,769 ulp | 9 ulp |

The case above is now exact. `stddev` is not correctly rounded; it is within
9 ulp on this corpus.

## E — over 32 arguments (low)

Past the 32-argument cap the evaluator reported `ABACO_ERR_PARSE`, though the
input parses, and 2.4.10 added `ABACO_ERR_ARITY` for wrong argument counts.
**Fix.** The cap reports `ABACO_ERR_ARITY`. `test_arity_cap` and `fuzz_eval`
now require that exact code; until now they accepted any error.

## G — `sinc_kernel`'s envelope (info)

The Hann envelope computed `cos((π·|x|) / hw)`.
- A subnormal half-width rounded π·|x| to a few ulps:
  `sinc_kernel(5e-324, 5e-324)` was 0.005, not 0.
- |x| past ~5.7e307 overflowed the product, giving NaN.

**Fix.** `cos(π · (|x| / hw))`. The ratio lies in [0, 1] and is exactly 1 at
the edge, and it still depends only on |x|, so k(x) = k(−x) bit for bit.
Three rows in `test_dsp` fail on 2.5.2 and pass now, and the existing rows
held.

## H — the absolute-zero margin (info)

The check allowed 1e-9 K below absolute zero, ~30,000 times the 3e-14 °C of
rounding it exists to absorb (−459.67 °F → −273.15000000000003 °C), so −1e-9 K
converted to a negative temperature. **Fix.** The margin is now 1e-12 K: −1e-9 K
and −5e-10 K are refused, while 0 K, −273.15 °C and −459.67 °F are still
accepted.

## Checked and sound

The reviewers' cross-checks, beyond the findings:

- **Literals:** ~357,000 hard literals matched `float()` bit for bit. They
  covered exact midpoints, ±1 in the last digit, cuts at 17–100 digits,
  subnormal ties, 2^-1075, and the overflow tie. 50 MB literals parse in
  linear time; the worst exact comparison costs ~23 µs.
- **Factorial, `tan`, `totient`, dispatch:**
  - `n!` for n = 0..170 is exact.
  - `tan` on 20,010 hard inputs is within 1 ulp of glibc (98% bit-equal).
  - The uncapped `totient` is correct at ~1 ms per hard case.
  - The builtin name and arity table equals 2.4.9's.
- **Evaluator robustness:** 120,000 fuzzed expressions ran with no crash;
  300,000 evaluations showed no memory growth; 8 threads with their own
  Evaluators gave 0 divergences.
- **Factorization:** 498 constructed cases and a 3,822-number sweep were exact
  on x86_64 and aarch64. A C replica of rho never needed more than 2
  polynomials in a row over 20 million semiprimes. The `pend[64]` and `big[32]`
  buffers are sized correctly.
- **Other number theory:** `abaco_binomial` matched `math.comb` on ~4,000
  pairs, `_isqrt` matched `math.isqrt` near 2^52, 2^53 and 3037000499, and
  `mod_pow` with negative exponents matched Python.
- **Windows:** every symmetric and periodic window is within 2.6e-16 of the
  exact value, and every bit-exact symmetry claim holds on both architectures.
  Kaiser's error is at the f64 conditioning limit, and the `_fill` functions
  write exactly `size` samples.
- **HTTPS request side:**
  - URL grammar: no CRLF or control-byte injection.
  - The request builder's lengths are exact.
  - Response framing: Content-Length duplicates, lists, signs and
    Transfer-Encoding are refused, and no read goes past `len`.
  - Deadline arithmetic: no overflow; the 10 s step and the 30 s fetch hold
    after the lookup (measured 10,040 ms and 30,002 ms).
- **HTTPS resources:** no fd or TLS-ctx leak on any exit path. The CA-file
  bound and the 300-certificate count match sigil's PEM walk. No per-call
  state is kept in globals.
